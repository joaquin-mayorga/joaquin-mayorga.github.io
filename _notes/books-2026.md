Bence Nanay, *Aesthetics: A Very Short Introduction*  
<small>&nbsp;</small><br>
<small>The book begins by providing a definition of aesthetics as a discipline: the study of sensory experiences. Reading that, I suspected that the rest of the book would review the main ideas expressed in a canonical bibliography of the discipline of aesthetics. Instead, it discusses what are and aren’t the characteristics of what the author calls the aesthetic experience. That discussion, I believe, can be summarized into a definition of aesthetic experience that states as follows: </small>
<small>&nbsp;</small>
<small>*An aesthetic experience is a pleasurable experience of paying open-ended and for-its-own-sake attention to the many surface and scene features of an object seen as beautiful, while at the same time paying attention to the qualities of this experience.* </small>
<small>&nbsp;</small>
<small>I enjoyed reading a collection of cool quotes that I won’t reproduce and a brief discussion of the role of the critic. The idea is that a good critic directs our attention to things that we would not have noted otherwise, as paying attention to those features can transform our (potentially aesthetic) experience of perceiving a work of art.</small>
