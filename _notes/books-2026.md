Bence Nanay, *Aesthetics: A Very Short Introduction*   
<small>  </small> <br> 
<small> The book begins by providing a definition of aesthetics as a discipline: the study of sensory experiences. Reading that, I suspected that the rest of the book would review the main ideas expressed in a canonical bibliography of the discipline of aesthetics. Instead, it discusses what are and aren’t the characteristics of what the author calls the aesthetic experience. That discussion, I believe, can be summarized into a definition of aesthetic experience that states as follows: </small> <br> 
<small> *Aesthetic experience is the pleasurable experience of paying open-ended and for-its-own-sake attention to the many surface and scene features of an object seen as beautiful, while at the same time paying attention to the qualities of this experience.* </small> <br> 
<small> Besides that summary and a collection of cool quotes that I won’t reproduce, one thing I found interesting about this book was a brief discussion of the role of the critic. The idea is that a good critic directs our attention to things that we would not have noted otherwise, as paying attention to those features can transform our (potentially aesthetic) experience of perceiving a work of art. </small>  <br>
